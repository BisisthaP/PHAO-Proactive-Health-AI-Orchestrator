# NG136 — Hypertension in adults: diagnosis and management (Summary)

> **Note:** This is a condensed, educational summary of NICE guideline NG136 for use
> by the PHAO demo system. It is not a substitute for the full official guideline.
> Always confirm against the current NICE publication before any clinical decision.

## Diagnosis and BP thresholds

Use clinic blood pressure (BP) measurement together with ambulatory blood pressure
monitoring (ABPM) or home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) to confirm a diagnosis of
hypertension.

- **Stage 1 hypertension:** clinic BP 140/90 mmHg or higher, and ABPM/HBPM average
  135/85 mmHg or higher.
- **Stage 2 hypertension:** clinic BP 160/100 mmHg or higher, and ABPM/HBPM average
  150/95 mmHg or higher.
- **Stage 3 (severe) hypertension:** clinic systolic BP 180 mmHg or higher, or clinic
  diastolic BP 120 mmHg or higher.

## When to offer drug treatment

Offer antihypertensive drug treatment in addition to lifestyle advice to adults of any
age with persistent stage 2 hypertension.

For adults under 80 with stage 1 hypertension, offer drug treatment if they have any of:
target organ damage, established cardiovascular disease, renal disease, diabetes, or a
10-year cardiovascular risk (QRISK) of 10% or more.

## Step 1 treatment

- Offer an ACE inhibitor or an angiotensin-II receptor blocker (ARB) to adults aged
  under 55 and to adults of any age with type 2 diabetes.
- Offer a calcium-channel blocker (CCB) to adults aged 55 or over without type 2
  diabetes, and to adults of Black African or African-Caribbean family origin of any age
  without type 2 diabetes.

## Step 2 and step 3

- Step 2: combine an ACE inhibitor or ARB with a CCB or a thiazide-like diuretic.
- Step 3: offer an ACE inhibitor or ARB plus a CCB plus a thiazide-like diuretic.

## Blood pressure treatment targets

- Adults under 80 years: clinic BP below 140/90 mmHg (ABPM/HBPM below 135/85 mmHg).
- Adults aged 80 and over: clinic BP below 150/90 mmHg (ABPM/HBPM below 145/85 mmHg).

## Monitoring and lifestyle

Offer lifestyle advice on diet, exercise, alcohol, smoking and reducing salt intake.
Review blood pressure and medication at least annually, and monitor for adverse effects.
